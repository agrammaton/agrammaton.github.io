I am Associate Professor in the Department of Electrical Engineering at the NUST School of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science (SEECS) in Islamabad.

I graduated with a BS in Electronics Engineering from GIK Institute of Engineering Sciences and Technology, followed by a Masters of Engineering Science from the University of New South Wales (UNSW). I did my PhD, also from UNSW, under the supervision of [Vijay Sivaraman](https://research.unsw.edu.au/people/professor-vijay-sivaraman) and [Diet Ostry](https://csiropedia.csiro.au/ostry-diethelm/). My research consisted of devising lightweight security solutions for bodyworn healthcare devices.

I have about three years industry experience, working at WorldCALL Broadband Ltd., in various engineering roles. As a researcher, I’ve worked at CSIRO and UNSW on projects including green WiFi, software-defined networks, and data forensics. I also did a research postdoc at Newcastle University, in [Feng Hao‘s](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/dcs/people/feng_hao/) group, where I worked on secure election protocols and cryptocurrencies.

Besides academia, I work with government and civil society organizations on technology policy, digital transformation, and institutional development.

I write regularly for local media ([DAWN](https://www.dawn.com/authors/6843/taha-ali), [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/author/4541/taha-ali), etc.).

I am also involved with the *In Plato's Cave* podcast.


## Research

My academic work has focused primarily on computer networks, information security, and emerging technologies. More specifically, I have worked on questions surrounding blockchain and cryptocurrencies, electronic voting, the Internet of Things, and machine learning.

An increasingly important part of my work concerns **technology policy and emerging technologies**. I am interested in how governments and institutions can respond intelligently to rapidly changing technologies while preserving security, accountability, innovation, and public trust.

My broader objective is to understand not only **what technologies can do**, but also **how they change the institutions and societies in which they are deployed**.
